**What held every model at ~80%:** the same games failed for all three. The LittleJS skill's own examples used `setCollision(true, false)` on both sides, which the engine never collides; nothing said collision boxes ignore `angle`; nothing asked for fair games; and Haiku spent its steps looking for skill files with bash, which can't see them. The fixed skills alone moved Haiku on the frozen specs from 78% to 79% (90 runs; step-limit fails 7 → 3). Coder effort high reached 88% on 40 runs but at a median of 103 s, so it was stopped.

**Round 2: the spec, end to end** (Coder: Haiku at low effort):

| Setup | Builds pass | Build median / p90 | Talker pass | First token | Reply |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Talker low, 60-word prompt | 81% | 56 / 110 s | 75% | 2.1 s | 5.8 s |

| Talker low, 40-word prompt | 78% | 56 / 81 s | 79% | 1.4 s | 5.6 s |
| Talker low + a high-effort designer call in `build` | 71% | 62 / 110 s | 71% | 1.3 s | 21 s |
| **Talker high**, 40-word prompt, failed-build rule (final) | 83% | 62 / 96 s | **92%** | 2.9 s | 7.0 s |

The Talker writing the spec at high effort is what lifts the builds. A designer call inside `build` made things worse (it rewrote "start over" as a change of the old game and added changes nobody asked for) and added ~15 s per build. Asking for replies under 40 words removed every brevity failure. Telling the Talker that a failed build leaves no game (so the next request is briefed as a complete new game) fixed the failures where it briefed a change to nothing.

## Judge calibration

The owner blind-scored 8 runs of round 1: 2 had the judge more than a point off (the judge softer on "reads as the spec", harsher on "playable"), within #115's limit. On 20 builds of the chosen setup, 7 of them ones the judge failed, the owner found all 20 very good; recorded as at least 4 on every item. The judge is stricter than the owner, so the 89% above is a floor, and the owner ruled that the pair clears. The 20 runs are listed in the JSON.

## Changes this made to earlier decisions

- **#115's Talker limits** loosened to a 3 s median first token and a 10 s median reply (owner).
- **#122's brief** is now a design spec of up to ~300 words (goal, rules with numbers, controls, difficulty, fairness, look, language), not ≤ ~120 words; the Talker still knows no engine. A failed build leaves no game: the Talker briefs a full game until one succeeds.
- **#122's limits**: the Coder's deadline is 120 s (150 s with music) and the Talker's abort timer 30 s later, replacing 4½ and 5 minutes.
- **#111's starting limits** (12 steps, 5 minutes) become 10 steps and 120 s.
- No new provider: both agents are Anthropic models through Cloudflare, so #120 and #125 gain nothing.

## Spend

The kept runs' own records add up to $34 at pi's list prices: $10 for the candidates and $24 for the Opus judge (about $0.03 per judged game), plus runs that were discarded or interrupted. The gateway refused requests (402, "insufficient balance") while ~8–15 were in flight and the dashboard still showed credit, and its charges ran ahead of the records at least once; the sweeps now stop on the first refusal and discard that run.
