# Game art: making the Coder's games look polished, 2026-10-21

Follow-up to #116: the chosen setup (Haiku 5.5 Coder at low effort, 12 steps, 120 s) built games that played well but looked like prototypes: flat rects and circles on one fill. This adds art guidance to the Coder's two skills and a "looks" score to the judge, and measures it on the existing suite.

**Result:** looks up from 2.6 to 3.1 (of 5) with pass rate up, not down, for a few seconds and under a cent more per build. The LittleJS sprites ship as **pixel art** (the owner's pick over canvas-drawn sprites and emoji). Contact sheets: [before](2026-10-21-art-before.jpg), [after, canvas sprites](2026-10-21-art-after.jpg), [LittleJS jobs with pixel art](2026-10-21-art-pixel.jpg), and [the same games three ways](2026-10-21-art-three-way.jpg).

## What changed

- **`littlejs-api` skill**: a "Make it look good" section (a palette, a gradient sky with parallax layers, outlines, soft shadows, highlights, outlined text; juice: palette-coloured particles, screen shake, squash and stretch, a pop) and **pixel-art sprites** for every character and object the player looks at: a text grid, one letter per pixel, 12–16 wide, with an outline and 2–3 shades per colour, painted once on an `OffscreenCanvas` and used as a crisp `TextureInfo` / `TileInfo`, drawn at 2.5–3.5 world units. The Sky Hopper example now has a pixel-art bird, parallax hills and outlined pipes. The old rule "draw everything procedurally" is gone.
- **`dom-games` skill**: a "Make it look good" section (palette as CSS variables, layered gradient backgrounds, 3D buttons and cards with shadows and a pressed state, drop-shadowed emoji, inline SVG, pop and shake keyframes, card flips), and a quiz example with a gradient and pressable buttons.
- **The judge** scores a fourth item, "looks" (1 = flat placeholder shapes on a plain fill, 3 = tidy but plain, 5 = could ship in a store), from the screenshots. **Reported only, not part of a pass**; the report shows its mean.

**Sprites and the CSP.** A canvas-drawn sprite never becomes a URL, so the game CSP (`img-src data: blob:`) doesn't apply; an inline SVG as a `data:image/svg+xml` image also loads under it, but asynchronously, so the skill paints the grid on a canvas instead. Both typecheck against the narrowed `littlejs.d.ts` (`TextureInfo`, `TileInfo` and `drawTile` are in the release build). Checked with the box's own playtest locally.

## Decisions for the owner

1. **Ship the art guidance?** Recommended: it lifts looks by about half a point with no loss in pass rate, at +6–13 s median and under $0.003 per build. Pixel art: chosen by the owner.
2. **Should "looks" join the pass bar?** Recommended: not yet. At ≥ 4 almost nothing passes today (the mean is 3.1), and the score isn't calibrated against the owner.
3. **A sprite pack** (below)?

## Option, not built: a curated CC0 sprite pack

Kenney's packs are CC0 1.0 (public domain): commercial use, no attribution required (credit optional; Kenney asks that its logo not be used). Zip sizes, fetched 2026-10-21:

| Pack | What | Files | Zip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tiny Dungeon | 16 px heroes, monsters, items, tiles | 130 | 99 KB |
| Pixel Platformer | 18 px characters, enemies, tiles, pickups | 200 | 260 KB |
| Shape Characters | vector-style bodies, faces and hands to combine |  | 546 KB |
| Animal Pack | round and square animal heads |  | 2.6 MB |
| Food Kit | food items |  | 4.6 MB |
| Particle Pack | smoke, sparks, stars, magic |  | 15 MB |

What it would take:

- **A curated atlas**, not whole packs: ~60–100 sprites the specs ask for most (a hero, animals, enemies, coins, gems, stars, hearts, fruit, rocks, clouds, a few tiles) in one PNG of ~100–250 KB. One pixel set and one smooth set, picked per game, so a game doesn't mix styles.
- **Serving it**: the game CSP allows images only from `data:` and `blob:`. Either add `img-src 'self'` and serve `/runtime/sprites-v1.png` beside `runtime.js` (a change to the served-runtime contract, #122, and the playtest's routes), or inline it in the runtime as a data: URL (+33%, paid on every game load).
- **Loading**: LittleJS takes images through `engineInit`, which the scaffold calls; the scaffold would pass the atlas and expose named tiles (`hopon.sprite("cat")`), listed in the skill and typed in the `.d.ts` so a wrong name fails the typecheck.
- **Cost to the Coder**: the sprite list in the skill (~1–2k tokens a job); no extra tool calls.
- **Against model-drawn sprites**: a pack looks consistently professional, but every game draws from the same ~100 pictures, and anything off the list (a moon cat, a gnome) is drawn by hand anyway. Model-drawn sprites fit any spec and cost nothing to serve, but look homemade.

Sources: [kenney.nl/support](https://kenney.nl/support) (license), [Tiny Dungeon](https://kenney.nl/assets/tiny-dungeon) and [Pixel Platformer](https://kenney.nl/assets/pixel-platformer) (file counts), zip sizes from the download links' `Content-Length`.
