# Game art: making the Coder's games look polished, 2026-10-21

Follow-up to #116: the chosen setup (Haiku 5.5 Coder at low effort, 12 steps, 120 s) built games that played well but looked like prototypes: flat rects and circles on one fill. This adds art guidance to the Coder's two skills and a "looks" score to the judge, and measures it on the existing suite.

**Result:** looks up from 2.6 to 3.1 (of 5) with pass rate up, not down, for a few seconds and under a cent more per build. The LittleJS sprites ship as **pixel art** (the owner's pick over canvas-drawn sprites and emoji). Contact sheets: [before](2026-10-21-art-before.jpg), [after, canvas sprites](2026-10-21-art-after.jpg), [LittleJS jobs with pixel art](2026-10-21-art-pixel.jpg), and [the same games three ways](2026-10-21-art-three-way.jpg).

## What changed

- **`littlejs-api` skill**: a "Make it look good" section (a palette, a gradient sky with parallax layers, outlines, soft shadows, highlights, outlined text; juice: palette-coloured particles, screen shake, squash and stretch, a pop) and **pixel-art sprites** for every character and object the player looks at: a text grid, one letter per pixel, 12–16 wide, with an outline and 2–3 shades per colour, painted once on an `OffscreenCanvas` and used as a crisp `TextureInfo` / `TileInfo`, drawn at 2.5–3.5 world units. The Sky Hopper example now has a pixel-art bird, parallax hills and outlined pipes. The old rule "draw everything procedurally" is gone.
- **`dom-games` skill**: a "Make it look good" section (palette as CSS variables, layered gradient backgrounds, 3D buttons and cards with shadows and a pressed state, drop-shadowed emoji, inline SVG, pop and shake keyframes, card flips), and a quiz example with a gradient and pressable buttons.
- **The judge** scores a fourth item, "looks" (1 = flat placeholder shapes on a plain fill, 3 = tidy but plain, 5 = could ship in a store), from the screenshots. **Reported only, not part of a pass**; the report shows its mean.

**Sprites and the CSP.** A canvas-drawn sprite never becomes a URL, so the game CSP (`img-src data: blob:`) doesn't apply; an inline SVG as a `data:image/svg+xml` image also loads under it, but asynchronously, so the skill paints the grid on a canvas instead. Both typecheck against the narrowed `littlejs.d.ts` (`TextureInfo`, `TileInfo` and `drawTile` are in the release build). Checked with the box's own playtest locally.

The cost is ~6 s more at the median and ~12 s at p90, still inside the 120 s deadline.

## Decided

The owner chose to ship the art guidance with pixel-art sprites. "Looks" stays a reported score, not part of a pass: the judge's looks scores aren't calibrated against the owner yet. A ready-made sprite pack is out of scope here.

## Spend

About $13 in the kept runs' records (Opus judge $11, Haiku $1.9), plus ~$4 in runs discarded: a first baseline at 10 steps, a third of the after-sweep cut short when the machine slept, and pixel-art runs that hung on a network outage. Gateway 402s stopped a sweep twice; resuming worked both times.
