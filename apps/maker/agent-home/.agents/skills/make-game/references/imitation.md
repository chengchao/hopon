# An imitation

Read this only when the Creator names an existing game to imitate or remake ("like Zelda", "a Tetris clone", "my own Stardew Valley"). A kind of game ("a roguelike", "a puzzle game") names no game, so it is not an imitation.

The Creator wants the game they know. A player who knows the named game should recognise this one from its first minute of play.

## Pillars

The design gains one part, **Pillars**, after Asked: what a player who knows the named game expects to find in this one, in 5 to 8 lines.

- the view, such as a side view of platforms, a top-down map of square tiles, or a board;
- what the player does from moment to moment, such as running and jumping, or planting and watering crops;
- the places the game moves between, such as a field, a village and a shop;
- the loop that joins them, such as plant, harvest, sell, buy better seed;
- what grows over a play, such as a level, a farm or a collection;
- how it looks, such as 16-bit pixel art;
- how its screens are laid out, and the style of its windows, menus and buttons.

The game keeps every pillar as something the player does and sees on screen. To make the game fit, make a pillar smaller: 3 levels where the named game has 30, one kind of enemy where it has twenty. Never drop a pillar, and never turn one into a menu or a line of text. A platformer where nobody jumps, or a farming game that is only its shop, is not the game the Creator asked for. When a pillar is something a hopon game can't do, such as saving, write which pillar, and what stands in for it.

## What is the named game's, and what is this game's

Take from the named game how it plays, how its screens are laid out, and the style of its windows, menus and buttons, as a player would remember them. Fit the layout to an upright phone and a finger: a list of commands keeps its place and its look, with rows tall enough to tap.

Give this game its own name, characters, places, story and pictures. Never use the named game's.

## The other parts

- **Screens:** a place in Pillars is a screen of its own, drawn as that place.
- **Look:** the style, the palette, and the look of the windows, menus and buttons follow the named game's.
- **Done when:** the criteria show the pillars first ("Swiping up makes the hero jump.", "Walking into the shop opens its counter.").
